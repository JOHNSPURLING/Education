## At a glance

- Formative assessment means using evidence of what pupils know to change what happens next in teaching, within the lesson or the next one. It is a process, not a type of test.
- Black and Wiliam's 'Inside the Black Box' (1998) made the case that it raises attainment; Leahy, Lyon, Thompson and Wiliam (2005) organised it into five key strategies.
- The EEF-funded trial of the Embedding Formative Assessment (EFA) programme in 140 English secondary schools found about two months' additional progress on Attainment 8.
- In England, "AfL" was widely distorted in the 2000s into sharing levels and objectives on the board. The five strategies are the corrective.
- The single most useful habit: plan the question or task that will tell you whether to move on, and decide in advance what you will do with each likely answer.

## What the evidence says

**The five strategies** (paraphrased from Leahy et al., 2005, and Wiliam's 'Embedded Formative Assessment', 2011; second edition 2018):

1. Clarify, share and understand learning intentions and success criteria.
2. Engineer discussions, tasks and activities that elicit evidence of learning.
3. Provide feedback that moves learners forward.
4. Activate pupils as learning resources for one another.
5. Activate pupils as owners of their own learning.

They come from crossing three questions (where the learner is going, where they are now, how to get there) with three agents (teacher, peer, learner).

**'Inside the Black Box' (1998)** summarised Black and Wiliam's research review and reported typical effect sizes of roughly 0.4 to 0.7 for formative assessment interventions. That is a range across heterogeneous studies, not a pooled estimate, and later work has been more cautious (see critiques).

**The EFA trial.** The programme, written by Dylan Wiliam and Siobhan Leahy and delivered by SSAT, is a two-year professional development model: monthly 75-minute Teacher Learning Community workshops for groups of 8–14 staff, with peer observation in between. The independent evaluation by NIESR (140 schools, about 25,000 pupils who began in Year 10 in 2015) found an effect size of 0.10 on Attainment 8, which the EEF converts to two months' progress and rates as very high security. The result was statistically significant only at the 10% level, and there was no evidence of improvement in English or maths GCSE specifically. The EEF lists EFA as a promising programme and has funded scale-up.

## In the classroom

- **Strategy 1.** Show pupils what quality looks like rather than reading out an objective. In Year 8 history, compare two anonymised paragraphs explaining causes of the Civil War and have pupils say which is better and why, before they write their own.
- **Strategy 2.** Use all-pupil response: mini-whiteboards for a balancing-equations question in chemistry; a multiple-choice hinge question answered with fingers in maths; cold call rather than hands up, with thinking time. Plan the question before the lesson.
- **Strategy 3.** Give feedback as a task: "Find and fix the two sentences where the tense slips" (MFL), rather than a grade and "check tenses".
- **Strategy 4.** Structured peer work: pupils swap PE performance checklists or use "two stars and a wish" only once they have been trained on a model; use "C3B4ME" (see three peers before the teacher) during independent practice.
- **Strategy 5.** Pupils flag confidence after a quiz, keep an error log in maths, or select which exam question type to practise from their own mock analysis.

Then respond: if a third of the class picks the same wrong option, reteach now; if three pupils do, pull them together while the rest practise.

## For leaders

- The trial tested a sustained, two-year, teacher-led programme. A single INSET day on "AfL strategies" is not the same intervention.
- Teacher Learning Communities need protected time (monthly meetings in directed time) and voluntary choice of techniques within the five strategies.
- Costs in the trial were low per pupil; the main cost is staff time.
- Do not monitor by checklist ("lolly sticks seen", "objectives on the board"). Look for whether teachers adjust teaching in response to evidence.
- Align with the feedback and marking policy so formative work in lessons replaces, not adds to, written marking.

## Cautions and critiques

- **Definition problems.** Randy Bennett's 2011 critical review argued that "formative assessment" is too loosely defined to have a single effect size and that the 0.4–0.7 claim is not well founded. Kingston and Nash's 2011 meta-analysis reported a much smaller average effect (around 0.2).
- **The letter without the spirit.** Research on England's national AfL rollout found many teachers adopted surface procedures (traffic lights, written objectives) without changing teaching. Wiliam has called the government's AfL strategy a missed opportunity.
- **Generic versus subject-specific.** Critics including Christodoulou argue formative assessment depends on a well-specified curriculum and good questions in each subject; generic techniques are not enough.
- **Modest trial effect.** 0.10 is small, though meaningful at whole-school scale and cheap. Schools should not expect dramatic gains.
- Self-reports of confidence (strategy 5) are unreliable guides to actual learning; pair them with a check.
