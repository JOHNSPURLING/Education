## At a glance

- Alternative provision (AP) is education arranged by a **local authority** for pupils who would otherwise not receive suitable education, or by a **school** for a suspended pupil or a pupil directed off site to improve behaviour.
- The DfE guidance is **'Arranging alternative provision: a guide for local authorities and schools'**, rewritten in **February 2025** to replace the 2013 version. It was still the reference text in 2026; the July 2026 exclusions guidance is to be read alongside it.
- The commissioning school **keeps responsibility** for a pupil it places: safeguarding, attendance, quality of education and outcomes.
- Providers range from PRUs and AP academies to registered independent schools, FE colleges and **unregistered** settings. Unregistered settings are not inspected; the checks fall to the commissioner.
- Government has said it will legislate for mandatory standards and time limits in unregistered AP; for now the national standards (August 2025) are **voluntary**.

## Status and current version

- **Status:** statutory guidance for local authorities and maintained schools. Academies are not bound in the same way, but legal commentary notes they are expected to take account of it, and Ofsted will judge them against the same practice.
- **Legal basis:** Education Act 1996 s.19 (local authority duty to arrange suitable education for children who, because of exclusion, illness or otherwise, would not receive it; full-time from the sixth school day of a permanent exclusion); Education and Inspections Act 2006 s.100 (schools arrange full-time education from day six of a suspension); the off-site direction power, extended to academies and PRUs from 26 July 2026 by the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026.
- **Version:** February 2025. Not verified: the exact GOV.UK landing page and whether minor amendments were made in 2026; GOV.UK could not be opened directly and the publication page did not appear in search results, so law firm summaries are cited instead.
- **Policy direction:** the 2023 SEND and AP Improvement Plan described AP as a three-tier service (targeted support in mainstream, time-limited placements, transitional placements). The government's 2025 response on unregistered AP proposed local authority approved lists, mandatory national standards and limits on time spent in unregistered settings 'when parliamentary time allows'. A 2026 consultation on education otherwise than at school was due a response in autumn 2026. Not verified: any outcome as at November 2026.

## What schools must do

- **Arrange suitable full-time education** from the sixth school day of a suspension.
- **Follow the off-site direction rules** when directing a pupil to AP for behaviour: written notice to parents, clear objectives, regular review involving parents, and time limits (see the linked entry).
- **Record attendance correctly.** A pupil at AP stays on the school roll; use the attendance codes for approved off-site education or dual registration, and obtain daily attendance from the provider.
- **Safeguard.** Keeping Children Safe in Education requires the school to obtain written confirmation that appropriate safeguarding checks have been carried out on staff at the provider, to know where the pupil is during school hours, and to review placements to check they are safe and meeting need.
- **Do not use an illegal school.** A setting providing full-time education to five or more pupils of compulsory school age, or one with an EHC plan or who is looked after, must be registered as a school.
- **Report AP placements in the school census.**

## In practice

A commissioning checklist for a secondary school:

1. **Purpose.** Write down what the placement is for and what success looks like: for example, 12 weeks, two days a week, to re-establish attendance and complete a Level 1 construction unit, returning to full timetable in January.
2. **Due diligence before placement:** registration status; latest inspection; safeguarding policy and named DSL; DBS and safer recruitment confirmation in writing; insurance; health and safety and risk assessments; curriculum offer including English and maths; staff qualifications; how behaviour and physical intervention are handled.
3. **Visit** before placing and at least termly, unannounced where possible. Talk to the pupil there.
4. **Share information:** needs, risk assessment, EHC plan, medical details, emergency contacts, who to phone if the pupil does not arrive.
5. **Agree in writing:** timetable, cost, daily attendance reporting by a set time, weekly progress update, incident reporting the same day, review dates.
6. **Keep the pupil connected:** a named member of staff, a weekly check-in, access to careers guidance, exam entries and school events.
7. **Plan the return** from day one. Reintegration needs a phased timetable, a key adult and teachers briefed on what has changed.

For leaders: keep a single register of every pupil educated off site, with provider, hours, cost, attendance and review dates; report it to governors termly with breakdowns by SEND, disadvantage and ethnicity; and check total hours add up to full time unless a part-time arrangement is justified and time-limited.

## Common pitfalls

- 'Out of sight' placements with no review date, which function as informal exclusion.
- Assuming the local authority or the provider has done the safeguarding checks.
- A mosaic of part-time unregistered placements that adds up to an unregistered school.
- A narrow curriculum that closes off GCSE English and maths.
- Attendance marked as present on the provider's say-so with no daily confirmation.
- Placing pupils whose underlying need is unassessed SEND; AP then substitutes for an EHC needs assessment.
