## At a glance

- Headteachers, and staff they authorise, have a **statutory power to search a pupil without consent** for a defined list of prohibited items, and for items the school rules identify as searchable.
- A without-consent search needs **reasonable grounds to suspect** the pupil has such an item.
- The DfE advice in force is the **July 2022** version (in effect from 1 September 2022). Schools' own 2025 and 2026 policies still cite it and no later edition was found on 12 November 2026.
- The 2022 rewrite followed the Child Q safeguarding review. It puts the pupil's welfare first, expects every search to be recorded and parents told, and sets out the school's role when **police** consider a strip search.
- School staff may only require removal of **outer clothing**. Staff never conduct a strip search.

## Status and current version

- **Status:** non-statutory advice explaining statutory powers. The powers are in the Education Act 1996 (ss.550ZA to 550ZD), the Education and Inspections Act 2006 (ss.91 and 94) and the Schools (Specification and Disposal of Articles) Regulations 2012.
- **Applies to:** all schools in England.
- **Version:** July 2022, replacing the 2018 edition. Main changes: safeguarding foregrounded; designated safeguarding lead (DSL) to be informed of searches; recording and parental notification expectations; a new section on strip searches by police; emphasis on analysing search data for disproportionality.
- Not verified: the GOV.UK page could not be opened directly to confirm its last-updated date. The 2026 mobile phones guidance refers schools to this document, which is consistent with it remaining current.

## What schools must do

Legal requirements for a search **without consent**:

- **Who:** the headteacher or a member of staff authorised by the head. The head can authorise individuals, groups, or all staff, but cannot compel a teacher to search (security staff aside).
- **Grounds:** reasonable suspicion that the pupil has a prohibited item.
- **Prohibited items in law:** knives and weapons; alcohol; illegal drugs; stolen items; tobacco and cigarette papers; fireworks; pornographic images; any article the staff member reasonably suspects has been, or is likely to be, used to commit an offence, injure someone or damage property. Plus any item the school rules ban and identify as one that may be searched for.
- **Sex and witness:** the searcher must be the same sex as the pupil, with another member of staff present as witness. The only exception is where there is risk of serious harm if the search is not carried out urgently and it is not reasonably practicable to comply.
- **Extent:** outer clothing, pockets, possessions, desks and lockers. Nothing beyond outer clothing may be required to be removed.
- **Where:** on school premises, or elsewhere when the member of staff has lawful control or charge of the pupil, such as on a trip.
- **Disposal:** controlled drugs, weapons, stolen items of value and suspected indecent images of children go to the police. Do not view or forward suspected indecent images of a child; pass the device to the DSL.

For items banned only by school rules and not designated as searchable, a search needs the pupil's agreement; refusal can be sanctioned under the behaviour policy.

## In practice

What the guidance says schools **should** do:

- **Before:** explain to the pupil why they are being searched, how and where, and give them the chance to ask questions. Seek co-operation first. Use a private place away from other pupils.
- **A workable script:** "I have reason to think you may have a vape in your bag, which our rules don't allow. Ms Khan and I are going to ask you to empty your pockets and bag in the office. You won't be asked to take off anything other than your blazer. Do you have anything you want to hand over first?"
- **If the pupil refuses:** assess the risk. For a suspected weapon, keep the pupil supervised and away from others and call the police rather than attempt a contested search. Reasonable force may lawfully be used to search for statutory prohibited items but not for items banned only by school rules; treat it as a last resort and follow the 2026 restrictive interventions guidance.
- **After:** record the search in the safeguarding system whether or not anything was found: date, time, location, who searched and witnessed, the pupil, the reason, what was looked for, what was found and follow-up. Inform the DSL without delay where there is a safeguarding concern. Tell parents as soon as practicable.
- **Electronic devices:** staff may examine data or files where there is good reason, and may delete material in some circumstances, but should involve the DSL first and must not delete possible evidence of a crime.
- **Screening:** schools can require pupils to pass through a metal detector or be wanded without consent or suspicion; this is not a search. A pupil who refuses can be refused entry, recorded as unauthorised absence rather than suspension.
- **Strip searches:** only police can carry these out, under PACE Code C. Before calling police in, consider less invasive options and the pupil's wellbeing. Staff should remain the pupil's advocate, ensure an appropriate adult is present except in urgent cases, inform parents, and record it as a safeguarding matter.
- **Monitoring:** the DSL and governors should review search data by ethnicity, sex, SEND and year group for patterns.

## Common pitfalls

- Searching on a hunch or as a blanket measure with no recorded grounds.
- An opposite-sex or unwitnessed search without the urgent-harm exception genuinely applying.
- Asking pupils to lift or remove clothing beyond outer garments.
- Treating phones, vapes or energy drinks as 'prohibited items' when the behaviour policy does not list them as searchable.
- Handing a pupil over to police and stepping back.
- Not recording searches that find nothing, which hides disproportionality.
