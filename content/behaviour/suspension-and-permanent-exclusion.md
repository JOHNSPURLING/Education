## At a glance

- Only the **headteacher** (or acting head) can suspend or permanently exclude, and only on **disciplinary** grounds. Sending a pupil home 'to cool off', or because the school cannot meet their needs, without recording a suspension is unlawful.
- A new edition of the statutory guidance, published on 26 May 2026, governs every suspension and permanent exclusion that takes effect **on or after 26 July 2026**. It replaces the August 2024 version.
- The 2026 edition separates the two sanctions: suspension may sometimes be necessary; **permanent exclusion is a last resort**.
- It also rewrites the 'pupil movement' sections (off-site direction, managed moves) after the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026, and adds a section on **safeguarding separation**.
- A pupil can be suspended for at most **45 school days in one academic year**.
- Decisions are judged on the civil standard (balance of probabilities) and on public law principles: lawful, rational, reasonable, fair and proportionate.

## Status and current version

- **Status:** statutory guidance. Headteachers, governing boards, academy trusts, local authorities and review panels must have regard to it. The procedural duties come from the Education Act 2002 (s.51A) and the School Discipline (Pupil Exclusions and Reviews) (England) Regulations 2012, as amended.
- **Applies to:** maintained schools, academies and free schools, alternative provision academies and PRUs. It does not apply to independent schools or to post-16 institutions other than school sixth forms.
- **Version:** July 2026 edition (published 26 May 2026). Exclusions that took effect before 26 July 2026 continue under the 2024 guidance.
- **What changed in 2026,** as reported by DfE-facing summaries and education law firms:
  - permanent exclusion is defined as removal from the school on disciplinary grounds only;
  - academies now have the same statutory power as maintained schools to direct a pupil off site to improve behaviour, and PRU management committees gain it too; parents should have two school days' notice;
  - managed moves are to be permanent from the start, with no trial period; a family's refusal of a managed move is not a ground for permanent exclusion;
  - new text on safeguarding separation, which is not a substitute for suspension while an incident is investigated;
  - clearer expectations on reintegration after suspension, cancelled exclusion, off-site direction or separation;
  - clarification on a further suspension or a permanent exclusion where an investigation produces new evidence;
  - designated teachers should contact the virtual school head as soon as possible when a looked-after child is at risk of suspension or exclusion;
  - stronger emphasis on Equality Act and SEND duties.
- Not verified: GOV.UK could not be opened directly when this entry was written. The list above is drawn from secondary summaries; check paragraph-level wording in the guidance itself before relying on it in a hearing.

## What schools must do

The following duties are long-standing in the 2012 Regulations and earlier editions. Not verified line by line against the July 2026 text; confirm before acting.

- **Notify parents without delay** of the suspension or exclusion, the reasons, its length, their right to make representations to the governing board and how. Follow up in writing.
- **Notify the local authority** without delay of every suspension and exclusion, and the social worker and virtual school head where the pupil has one.
- **Set and mark work** for the first five school days. From the **sixth** school day, full-time alternative education must be arranged: by the school for a suspension, by the local authority for a permanent exclusion.
- **Governing board review.** The board must meet within 15 school days to consider reinstatement for a permanent exclusion, for suspensions taking the pupil over 15 school days in a term, or where a pupil would miss a public exam. For totals of more than 5 and up to 15 days in a term it must meet if parents make representations. For shorter totals it must consider any representations but cannot reinstate.
- **Independent review panel.** After a board upholds a permanent exclusion, parents have 15 school days to apply for an independent review and may ask for a SEN expert. The panel can uphold, recommend reconsideration, or quash and direct reconsideration.
- **Cancellation.** The head may cancel (withdraw) an exclusion that the governing board has not yet reviewed; parents, the board, the local authority and relevant professionals must then be told and the pupil allowed back.
- **Do not discriminate.** Disabled pupils are protected by the Equality Act 2010; claims go to the First-tier Tribunal (SEND).
- **Never exclude informally** or pressure parents to remove a child (off-rolling).

## In practice

- **Before deciding,** establish the facts, hear the pupil's account, and record evidence and reasoning at the time. Consider contributing factors: bereavement, bullying, unmet SEND, safeguarding concerns.
- **For permanent exclusion,** the established two-part test is a serious breach or persistent breaches of the behaviour policy, and that allowing the pupil to remain would seriously harm the education or welfare of the pupil or others. The decision letter should address both parts.
- **Pupils with an EHC plan or a social worker, and looked-after children:** contact the local authority, virtual school head or social worker early, and consider an emergency annual review, before exclusion becomes likely.
- **Reintegration.** Hold a short planned meeting on return: what happened, what support is in place, what the pupil will do differently, who checks in and when. Return to school should not be made conditional on parents attending.
- **Governors** need at least three members with no prior involvement, trained, with papers circulated in advance (five school days is the usual expectation).
- **Data.** Report suspensions and exclusions to governors termly by year group, SEND, free school meal eligibility, ethnicity and sex, alongside removals, off-site directions and managed moves, so that patterns of pupil movement are visible.

## Common pitfalls

- Suspensions without an end date, or extended rather than a fresh decision being taken.
- Vague reasons ('persistent disruptive behaviour') with no chronology of incidents and support.
- Part-time timetables, long removal or 'safeguarding separation' used to avoid the exclusion process.
- Decision letters that omit representation rights or deadlines.
- Treating a managed move as a trial, or presenting it as the only alternative to exclusion.
- Governing boards rubber-stamping; panels expect to see genuine scrutiny of proportionality and of SEND.
