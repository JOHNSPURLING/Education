## At a glance

- The 2013 advice 'Use of reasonable force' is no longer in force. Since **1 April 2026** schools follow 'Restrictive interventions, including use of reasonable force, in schools'.
- The legal power is unchanged: any member of school staff may use **reasonable force** to prevent a pupil committing an offence, injuring themselves or others, damaging property, or prejudicing good order and discipline (Education and Inspections Act 2006, s.93).
- What is new is a **legal duty to record and to report to parents** each significant incident in which force is used, and each incident of non-force restraint or seclusion.
- The guidance widens the frame from 'force' to **restrictive interventions**: physical restraint, non-physical restraint and seclusion. These must not be used as punishment or as routine behaviour management.
- Force must be proportionate, the minimum needed, and for the shortest time. Prevention and de-escalation come first.

## Status and current version

- **Title:** Restrictive interventions, including use of reasonable force, in schools (published December 2025, effective 1 April 2026).
- **Status:** mostly non-statutory advice. One section is **statutory guidance** issued under section 93A of the Education and Inspections Act 2006, covering recording and reporting. The entry's status field reflects the bulk of the document.
- **Legal basis for the new duties:** section 93A (significant incidents of force), commenced for April 2026, and the Schools (Recording and Reporting of Seclusion and Restraint) (No. 2) (England) Regulations 2025, which apply equivalent duties to seclusion and to restraint not involving force.
- **Applies to:** schools in England. Not verified: the precise list of school types to which each duty applies; check the guidance's status section.
- **Background:** a 2023 call for evidence and a 2025 consultation preceded the change. Disability organisations had campaigned for mandatory recording; the EHRC had recommended it in 2021.

## What schools must do

- **Record** every significant incident of force, and every incident of seclusion or non-force restraint, as soon as practicable. The guidance expects this the same day.
- **Report to parents** in writing as soon as practicable; guidance expects the same day. Exceptions exist, including where the pupil is aged 20 or over and where reporting would risk significant harm to the pupil (in which case the local authority is told instead; not verified against the regulations' exact wording).
- **Governing bodies and proprietors** must take reasonable steps to ensure the procedure is followed.
- **Never use force as punishment.** Corporal punishment is unlawful (Education Act 1996, s.548).
- **Meet Equality Act duties:** anticipate and make reasonable adjustments for disabled pupils, and consider the public sector equality duty when monitoring.
- **Follow Keeping Children Safe in Education** when an allegation is made about a member of staff's use of force.

'Significant incident' is not tightly defined. Commentators summarise it as force going beyond ordinary appropriate physical contact, and note that judgement is left to schools. When unsure, record.

## In practice

- **Policy.** Have a restrictive interventions policy or section of the behaviour policy: definitions, who is trained, when interventions may be used, recording, parental reporting, debrief, complaints and governor oversight. The old phrase 'positive handling' on its own no longer covers the ground.
- **Prevention first.** Know triggers; use the de-escalation steps in the linked entry; change adult, reduce audience, give space and time.
- **If force is needed,** for example separating two Year 10 pupils fighting on a corridor: call for help, give a clear verbal warning ("I'm going to hold your arm and move you back"), use the least intrusive hold, release as soon as the risk passes, then check both pupils for injury and distress.
- **Never** use holds that restrict breathing, prone or seated holds that risk positional asphyxia, or pain-inducing techniques.
- **Afterwards:** medical check; record (who, where, when, what led to it, what was tried first, what intervention, how long, injuries, witnesses); written report to parents; debrief with the pupil and with staff; DSL informed.
- **Seclusion** means confining a pupil alone and preventing them leaving, as a safety measure. It is different from a supervised removal room or a pupil choosing a calm space. A locked or held door falls within the recording duty and risks unlawful deprivation of liberty.
- **Individual plans.** For pupils where interventions are foreseeable, agree a support or risk-reduction plan with parents and, where relevant, the SENCO and health professionals. Review after every incident.
- **Training.** The guidance expects training proportionate to need; mainstream secondaries usually train a core team (pastoral, SEND, PE, on-call) in an accredited approach and brief all staff on the law and on de-escalation.
- **Governors** should receive termly anonymised data broken down by SEND, ethnicity, sex, year and location, and ask what is being done to reduce use.

## Common pitfalls

- Still working from the 2013 document or a pre-2026 'positive handling' policy.
- 'No touch' policies. The DfE has long advised against them; they leave staff unable to protect pupils.
- Recording only incidents with injuries, or reporting to parents by phone with nothing in writing.
- Calling seclusion 'time out', 'reflection' or 'withdrawal' and not recording it. Unions (reported in Tes) have said the definitions do not separate these clearly enough; leaders should define terms locally.
- Force used to enforce compliance with an instruction where there is no risk, for example to remove a seated, refusing pupil. Lawful power exists for good order, but proportionality is hard to show.
- No analysis of repeat use with the same pupil.
