## At a glance

- Part four of KCSIE has two sections: allegations that **may meet the harm threshold**, and concerns that do not (**low-level concerns**).
- It covers everyone working in the school: teachers, support staff, supply staff, volunteers and contractors. KCSIE 2026 adds **trainee teachers** throughout and is clearer about shared responsibility with agencies and other third parties.
- Threshold allegations go to the headteacher (or chair of governors if about the head) and are discussed with the **local authority designated officer (LADO)** before any investigation.
- 'Low-level' does not mean insignificant: it means behaviour inconsistent with the code of conduct that does not meet the harm threshold. All such concerns should be shared and recorded.
- Schools have a **legal duty** to refer to the Disclosure and Barring Service when someone is removed from regulated activity (or would have been had they not left) because of harm or risk of harm to a child.

## Status and current version

Statutory guidance: KCSIE 2026, Part four, in force 1 September 2026. Applies to all schools and colleges.

**What changed in 2026** (from secondary briefings; confirm against the text): trainee teachers are referenced throughout; the text makes clearer that schools share safeguarding responsibility with third-party organisations such as supply agencies. Not verified: any change to the low-level concerns wording in 2026.

**The harm threshold.** An allegation meets it where a person has:

- behaved in a way that has harmed or may have harmed a child;
- possibly committed a criminal offence against or related to a child;
- behaved towards a child in a way that indicates they may pose a risk of harm; or
- behaved, or may have behaved, in a way that indicates they may not be suitable to work with children (which covers conduct outside school, sometimes called transferable risk).

## What schools must do

**Must (legal duties):**

- refer to the DBS where the conditions above are met;
- consider referral to the Teaching Regulation Agency where a teacher is dismissed, or would have been, for serious misconduct;
- comply with reporting restrictions: it is an offence to publish material identifying a teacher subject to an allegation by a pupil at the school before charge.

**Should (KCSIE):**

- have procedures for both tiers, referenced in the child protection policy and staff code of conduct;
- make an immediate check that children are safe, then contact the LADO; the conventional expectation in local procedures is within one working day;
- not investigate or tell the accused before speaking to the LADO, and to the police where a crime may have been committed;
- appoint a case manager (normally the head); consider alternatives before suspension and record the rationale;
- record the outcome using KCSIE's definitions: substantiated, malicious, false, unsubstantiated or unfounded;
- keep a clear, comprehensive record on the personnel file (except for malicious or false allegations) and give a copy to the person; retain at least until normal pension age or ten years from the allegation if longer;
- not use settlement agreements to prevent referrals, and include substantiated safeguarding allegations in references;
- for supply staff, lead the process with the agency involved rather than simply ending the booking.

## In practice

**Staff:**

- report any concern about an adult's conduct, however small, to the head (or the person named in the policy); self-report if you find yourself in a situation open to misinterpretation;
- if the concern is about the head, go to the chair of governors or trust; if you feel unable to raise it internally, use whistleblowing routes, including the NSPCC whistleblowing helpline;
- do not discuss with colleagues or pupils.

**Low-level concerns** (examples in KCSIE include being over-friendly, having favourites, photographing pupils on a personal phone, one-to-one contact behind a closed door, humiliating language):

- record each concern in writing with context and action taken; hold records centrally and securely;
- review regularly for patterns across one person and across the culture;
- respond proportionately, usually a conversation and clarified expectations;
- consult the LADO if unsure whether the threshold is met.

**DSL:** is normally informed and advises on child protection aspects, even though the head manages the case. Make sure the child and parents are supported and kept informed within confidentiality limits.

**Governors and trustees:** the chair must know how to contact the LADO; the board should receive anonymised numbers of allegations and low-level concerns, timeliness and themes, and check that learning reviews happen after substantiated cases.

## Common pitfalls

- Investigating first and calling the LADO afterwards.
- Automatic suspension, or no recorded consideration of alternatives.
- Accepting a resignation and stopping the process; the investigation and any DBS referral should still follow.
- No low-level concerns system, or one that staff see as punitive so nothing is reported.
- Records scattered across emails; patterns missed.
- Supply or contractor concerns handled by 'do not send again'.
- Conduct outside school ignored.

## Go deeper

Your local safeguarding partnership's managing-allegations procedure sets LADO contact routes and timescales and must be followed. This is general information, not legal advice; take HR and legal advice on individual cases.
