## At a glance

- KCSIE says governing bodies and proprietors **should** ensure there is an effective child protection policy that reflects local safeguarding partnership procedures, is **reviewed at least annually** and is **publicly available**, normally on the website. For independent schools and academies the policy is a requirement of the independent school standards.
- It sits with a set of linked documents: staff behaviour policy (code of conduct), behaviour policy, safeguarding response to children absent from education, online safety, and the role of the DSL.
- The policy should describe what actually happens in **your** school: names, routes, systems, local contacts.
- For September 2026 the policy needed updating for KCSIE 2026, 'Working together' 2026 and the new information sharing duty.
- A policy is tested by whether a supply teacher, a lunchtime supervisor and a Year 8 pupil know what to do.

## What the evidence says

The requirement comes from statutory guidance, underpinned by section 175 of the Education Act 2002 (maintained schools and colleges) and section 157 with the independent school standards (academies and independent schools). In a multi-academy trust there may be a trust-wide policy, but each school needs its own local procedures and contacts.

**Content KCSIE expects the policy or linked policies to cover:**

- the school's commitment and the definition of safeguarding;
- roles: all staff, DSL and deputies, headteacher, governing body or trust;
- recognising abuse, neglect and exploitation, and specific issues such as child-on-child abuse, online safety, child sexual and criminal exploitation, FGM, mental health, Prevent;
- what to do when a child discloses or a concern arises; recording; when and how to refer; the right of any member of staff to refer directly;
- early help and Family Help;
- children with additional vulnerability: SEND, children with a social worker, looked-after and previously looked-after children, children absent from education, children in alternative provision;
- procedures for child-on-child abuse, including sharing of nudes;
- allegations and low-level concerns about adults; whistleblowing;
- safer recruitment (often a separate policy);
- filtering and monitoring, and use of mobile and smart technology;
- information sharing and record transfer;
- training and induction;
- use of school premises by other organisations: KCSIE says schools should seek assurance that hirers have safeguarding policies and that this is a condition of the hire.

**2026 points to reflect** (see the KCSIE entry; confirm against the text): Part one for all staff; Family Help terminology; gender questioning children; generative AI, deepfakes and annual filtering and monitoring review; DSL cover; alternative provision quality assurance; revised regulated activity; the information sharing duty.

## In the classroom

What the policy should mean for staff day to day:

- At induction every member of staff, including supply staff, trainees and volunteers, is given the policy, the code of conduct, the behaviour policy, the response to children absent from education, Part one of KCSIE and the names of the DSL team.
- A one-page flowchart in the staffroom, on lanyards or on the staff intranet: notice, reassure, record, report to DSL, same day.
- Staff know the scripts: 'I'm glad you told me. I can't keep this secret; I need to tell [DSL] so we can help.'
- Staff know the alternatives: head for concerns about adults; chair of governors for concerns about the head; children's social care or police directly if a child is in immediate danger.

## For leaders

**Writing it:**

- Start from your local authority or partnership model policy where one exists; it will carry the correct local thresholds and contacts. Then cut and localise.
- Keep the core procedure short and put reference material (definitions, indicators of specific harms) in appendices.
- Name roles, with contact details, and say who covers.
- Cross-check for contradictions with the behaviour, attendance, online safety, RSHE and staff conduct policies.
- Publish it; produce a pupil-friendly and parent-friendly summary.

**Making it work:**

- Brief all staff every September on what has changed; test with scenario questions through the year.
- Ask pupils whether they know who to talk to and whether they would.
- Include the policy in contracts with alternative provision, sports coaches, agencies and site hirers.

**Governors and trustees check:**

- approval and annual review date, and that it cites current guidance and local procedures;
- a named safeguarding governor or trustee;
- evidence of implementation: training records, case file audit, single central record check, pupil and staff voice, the local authority's annual safeguarding audit (section 175/157);
- that the board itself has had safeguarding training, which KCSIE says all governors and trustees should receive at induction and regularly thereafter.

## Cautions and critiques

Common audit and inspection failings:

- policy cites an out-of-date KCSIE edition, former staff or a previous local authority's contacts;
- a generic purchased template nobody on site has edited;
- trust policy with no school-level procedures;
- policy says one thing, staff describe another;
- no reference to site hirers, alternative provision or online learning;
- reviewed annually on paper but not after an incident or a change in law.

Length is a real problem: many policies exceed 40 pages and go unread. A short procedure plus appendices is defensible.

## Go deeper

Use your local safeguarding partnership's model policy and procedures first; they are the procedures you must follow. This is general information, not legal advice.
