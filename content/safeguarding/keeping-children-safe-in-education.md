## At a glance

- KCSIE is the DfE's statutory safeguarding guidance for schools and colleges in England. Governing bodies, proprietors and trusts **must** have regard to it.
- The version in force is **KCSIE 2026**, effective from **1 September 2026**. It replaced KCSIE 2025, which had made only technical changes.
- The 2026 edition was consulted on (February to April 2026), published in final form in July 2026 and is described by commentators as the most significant revision for several years.
- The condensed annex that some staff could read instead of Part one has been withdrawn: all staff are now expected to read **Part one in full**.
- Guidance on children questioning their gender is now inside KCSIE rather than a separate document.
- Local procedures still apply: KCSIE sits alongside 'Working together to safeguard children' and your local safeguarding partnership's arrangements.

## Status and current version

**Who it applies to.** Maintained schools, academies, free schools, independent schools, non-maintained special schools, pupil referral units, further education and sixth-form colleges, and other 16–19 providers covered by the guidance. It covers children up to 18.

**Version.** KCSIE 2026, in force 1 September 2026. A 'for information' version was released in July 2026 so schools could prepare. Not verified: whether the DfE made any further corrections to the text after July; check the GOV.UK publication page for the update history before quoting paragraph numbers. Paragraph numbering in the final text differs from the consultation draft, so citations taken from spring 2026 commentary may be wrong.

**Structure.** Part one (information for all staff); Part two (the management of safeguarding); Part three (safer recruitment); Part four (allegations and low-level concerns about staff); Part five (child-on-child sexual violence and sexual harassment); annexes, including further information on specific harms, the DSL role description and a summary of changes. Annex letters have shifted from 2025, so check references carried over in old policies.

**What changed in 2026** (as reported in the DfE's summary of changes and the NSPCC and legal briefings; confirm detail against the text):

- **Part one for everyone.** The condensed version of Part one is gone. A short overview may be used as a prompt but does not replace reading Part one.
- **Alignment with 'Working together' 2026**, including Family Help terminology and updated descriptions of abuse and neglect (for example, verbal abuse within emotional abuse).
- **Gender questioning children.** A new section of roughly 30 paragraphs on requests for social transition, parental involvement and single-sex spaces.
- **Online safety and AI.** New paragraphs on generative AI, explicit reference to deepfakes and 'nudification' tools, mobile phone policy brought into the online safety content, and an expectation that filtering and monitoring is reviewed at least annually and the review documented.
- **DSL role.** Stronger expectations on skills, experience and cover arrangements.
- **Alternative provision.** More emphasis on due diligence and ongoing quality assurance of placements.
- **Safer recruitment.** The definition of regulated activity is revised following the Crime and Policing Act 2026, which removes the 'supervision' exemption, bringing more supervised volunteers into scope of barred-list checks. Not verified: the commencement date of that statutory change.
- **Other additions** reported include prejudice-based harm, mental health support, young carers, premises, a revised serious violence section and notification about children in temporary accommodation.
- **Part five** expanded, with terminology aligned to 'Behaviour in schools'.

## What schools must do

Statutory duties sit in legislation (for example section 175 of the Education Act 2002 and the independent school standards); KCSIE explains how to meet them. Governing bodies and proprietors **must** have regard to the guidance, which means following it unless there is good reason not to. Within that framework KCSIE says schools **should**:

- ensure all staff read Part one, and that leaders and those working directly with children read the further-information annex;
- appoint a senior leader as designated safeguarding lead with appropriate status, time and resources;
- have an effective child protection policy, a staff behaviour policy (code of conduct) and arrangements for children who are absent from education;
- operate safer recruitment and maintain the single central record (the record itself is a legal requirement);
- have procedures for allegations and low-level concerns;
- provide safeguarding training at induction with regular updates;
- have appropriate filtering and monitoring.

## In practice

**Staff:** read Part one, know the DSL and deputies, report every concern promptly and in writing, never promise confidentiality, and know that anyone can make a referral to children's social care.

**DSLs:** complete a gap analysis of policies, training and the single central record against the 2026 text; brief staff on what is new rather than re-issuing the whole document; keep evidence that staff have read and understood Part one.

**Governors and trustees:** ask for the gap analysis and action plan; check the child protection policy cites KCSIE 2026; test, do not just receive, assurance on filtering and monitoring, the single central record, alternative provision checks and volunteer checks.

## Common pitfalls

- Policies that still cite an earlier edition, an old annex letter or superseded terminology.
- Recording that staff have 'signed for' KCSIE without checking understanding.
- Treating 'should' as optional: inspectors and reviewers expect a recorded rationale for any departure.
- Relying on consultation-draft summaries for paragraph references.
- Applying national guidance without the local partnership's thresholds and referral routes.
