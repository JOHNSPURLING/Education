## At a glance

- KCSIE frames online risk as the four Cs: **content, contact, conduct and commerce**.
- Governing bodies and proprietors **should** ensure appropriate filtering and monitoring is in place and its effectiveness regularly reviewed. KCSIE points to the DfE's **filtering and monitoring standards** (part of 'Meeting digital and technology standards in schools and colleges') as the benchmark.
- The DSL holds lead responsibility for online safety, including understanding the filtering and monitoring systems. IT staff or the provider run the system; they do not own the safeguarding decision.
- 2026 updates add generative AI: can your system deal with AI-generated and dynamic content, and have you risk-assessed AI tools before introducing them?
- Filtering blocks; monitoring watches. You need both, and a named person who reviews and acts on monitoring alerts.

## Status and current version

- **KCSIE 2026** (statutory, in force 1 September 2026). Online safety sits in Part two. 2026 changes reported: new paragraphs on generative AI, explicit mention of deepfakes and 'nudification' tools, mobile phone policy added to the online safety content, and the review of filtering and monitoring to be at least annual and documented.
- **DfE filtering and monitoring standards** (non-statutory standards that KCSIE tells schools to have regard to; first published March 2023). Updated in 2026 to add AI considerations. Not verified: the date of the latest update; secondary sources give both June and September 2026, so check the GOV.UK page.
- **UK Safer Internet Centre** publishes definitions of 'appropriate' filtering and monitoring and provider self-certification responses, revised in 2026.
- **Online Safety Act 2023.** Duties fall on platforms and search services and are enforced by Ofcom; the illegal content duties and children's safety duties (including age assurance for pornography) took effect in stages during 2025. The Act places no direct duty on schools and does not replace school filtering. It matters to schools for reporting routes, the newer communications and intimate-image offences, and realistic messages to pupils and parents.

The Prevent duty separately requires protection from terrorist and extremist material online.

## What schools must do

There is no statute that says 'install a filter', but schools **must** have regard to KCSIE and comply with the Prevent duty and data protection law. The DfE standards say schools **should**:

1. **Identify and assign roles and responsibilities.** A senior leader and a governor responsible; the DSL leading on safeguarding; IT staff or the provider on the technical side.
2. **Review provision at least annually**, and when a safeguarding risk is identified, working practice changes or new technology (including AI tools) is introduced.
3. **Block harmful and inappropriate content without unreasonably affecting teaching and learning.** The filter should be a member of the Internet Watch Foundation, use the counter-terrorism referral unit list, and block illegal child sexual abuse content.
4. **Have effective monitoring strategies** that meet the school's safeguarding needs, with alerts reviewed promptly by trained staff.

KCSIE also says schools should have an online safety approach reflected in the child protection policy, train staff (including on filtering and monitoring) at induction, teach online safety, and consider meeting the DfE cyber security standards.

## In practice

**Staff:** know what the acceptable use policy says; report anything a pupil reaches that should have been blocked, and any site wrongly blocked; supervise screens in lessons; treat a monitoring alert like any other concern.

**DSLs:**

- hold a written annual review, done with IT and a governor, covering: who is filtered (pupils, staff, guests, by age group), on which devices (school devices off site, bring-your-own, mobile data), what is logged, who gets alerts and how fast;
- run and log regular test checks from different user accounts and devices;
- make sure alerts are triaged daily in term time and that there is an arrangement for holidays where devices go home;
- update the data protection impact assessment and privacy notice when monitoring changes;
- ask the provider how the system handles generative AI tools and encrypted or app-based traffic.

**Governors and trustees:** see the annual review; ask what the last three monitoring alerts were and what happened; check the roles are named; confirm online safety is covered in training and the curriculum.

## Common pitfalls

- Buying a product and assuming compliance: the standards are about roles, review and response.
- Alerts going to an IT inbox that no safeguarding-trained person reads.
- Over-blocking that stops legitimate teaching in RSHE, history or science.
- No coverage for staff accounts, sixth-form own devices or school laptops at home.
- Pupils on mobile data: the filter is irrelevant, so the phone policy and education matter.
- Checks carried out but not recorded.
- Monitoring introduced without telling pupils, parents and staff what is monitored and why.
