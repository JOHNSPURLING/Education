## At a glance

- 'Working together to safeguard children' is the statutory guidance on how agencies in England work together to help, support and protect children.
- The current edition is **Working together 2026**, published by the DfE in March 2026 (secondary sources give 18 March). It replaces the 2023 edition.
- Its headline change is **Family Help**: targeted early help and section 17 'child in need' support are brought together as one offer, with a named lead practitioner for the family.
- It applies to education providers. KCSIE 2026 was rewritten to align with it, so school policies should use its terminology.
- Local detail (thresholds, referral forms, escalation routes) comes from the three statutory safeguarding partners: the local authority, the integrated care board and the police.

## Status and current version

**Who it applies to.** All organisations and agencies with functions relating to children, including local authorities, health bodies, police, and all schools, colleges and childcare settings. It is issued under several statutory powers (including the Children Acts 1989 and 2004); bodies covered must have regard to it and depart from it only with good reason.

**Version.** 2026 edition, replacing 2023. The DfE published a summary of changes and updated the Children's Social Care National Framework alongside it.

**What changed in 2026** (from the DfE summary and NSPCC and legal briefings):

- **Family Help** set out as a distinct tier, delivered by multi-disciplinary teams, reflecting the national 'Families First' reforms to children's social care.
- **Scope made explicit:** all children, including those in kinship care, under special guardianship, adopted or looked after, and unborn children where there are concerns.
- **New or strengthened themes:** recognising and challenging racism and discrimination; the needs of babies; domestic abuse; children facing several harms at once; hearing the child's voice through direct work, including non-verbal children.
- **Serious incident notifications and reviews:** changes to notification requirements and timescales for rapid reviews. Not verified: the exact timescales; check the text.
- **Education and childcare:** a dedicated section on partnership working with education and early years providers.
- **Unchanged:** the chapter on child death reviews.

**Related legislation.** The Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 contains measures to strengthen the role of education in local multi-agency safeguarding arrangements and to create multi-agency child protection teams. Not verified: whether those sections had been commenced by November 2026. See the entry on the Act.

## What schools must do

- Schools are named 'relevant agencies' in most local arrangements. When named, they **must** act in accordance with the local arrangements published by the safeguarding partners.
- Schools **must** have regard to this guidance and to KCSIE in carrying out their safeguarding duties.
- Schools **should** contribute to assessments, strategy discussions, child protection conferences, core groups and child-in-need or Family Help plans, and share relevant information.
- Schools **should** supply information for, and act on learning from, local child safeguarding practice reviews.
- Schools **must** tell the local authority when removing a pupil's name from the admission register at a non-standard transition point; this is a duty under the school attendance regulations, restated in the guidance.

## In practice

**Staff** do not need to read the full document. They need to know: what early help and Family Help are, that concerns go to the DSL, and that anyone may refer to children's social care directly if a child is at risk and the DSL cannot be reached.

**DSLs** should:

- read the chapters on Family Help, child protection and the education section, and the local partnership's updated threshold document;
- know the local 'front door' (often a MASH), the expected response time to a referral, and the local escalation or professional-disagreement procedure;
- attend or send a written report to every conference and core group, and keep evidence of contribution;
- be clear who the lead practitioner is for each child with a plan, and what the school's actions are.

**Governors and trustees** should check that the school is engaged with the local partnership (for example completing the section 175/157 safeguarding audit), that the policy reflects local procedures, and that the DSL reports on referral outcomes and escalations, not just counts.

## Common pitfalls

- Referrals that describe incidents but not the impact on the child or what the school has already tried.
- Accepting 'no further action' on a referral without following up or using the escalation procedure when concerns remain.
- Policies using pre-2026 terms or a superseded local threshold document.
- Poor attendance at multi-agency meetings in school holidays, with no report sent.
- Assuming national guidance is enough: local arrangements differ and are what partners and inspectors will test against.
