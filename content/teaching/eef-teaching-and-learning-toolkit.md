## At a glance

- The Toolkit summarises international research on about 30 approaches ('strands'), from feedback and homework to setting, repeating a year and school uniform.
- Each strand carries three ratings: **impact** (additional months of progress), **cost** (pound signs) and **evidence strength** (padlocks).
- The months figure is an *average across many studies* of different programmes, ages and countries. It is a starting point for a decision, not a prediction for your school.
- Read the text under each headline: it gives the breakdown by phase and subject, what the effective versions had in common, and what implementation costs.
- Since 2025 the EEF has described the Toolkit as a 'living' review, with strands re-searched and updated on a rolling basis, so figures change. Check the live page before quoting a number.
- It was designed for decisions about spending, particularly the pupil premium. It tells you 'what has worked on average', not 'what to do on Monday'.

## What the evidence says

**What it is.** The Toolkit began in 2011 as a Sutton Trust commission written by Steve Higgins and colleagues at Durham University, and became the EEF's flagship resource. Each strand pools the results of studies that meet set inclusion criteria and reports an average effect.

**Months of progress.** Studies report effect sizes (standardised mean differences). The EEF converts the pooled effect size into 'additional months of progress': how much further, on average, pupils receiving the approach moved in a year than similar pupils who did not. The conversion uses the progress a typical pupil makes in a year as the yardstick. It is a communication device rather than a literal measurement: nobody timed pupils' progress in months.

**Padlocks.** The padlock rating (0–5) reflects how much trust to place in the average. It is driven first by the number of studies meeting the criteria, and padlocks are then removed for weaknesses such as few recent studies, few randomised trials, a high share of researcher-designed tests, or large unexplained variation between studies. Strands with too few eligible studies get no months figure at all. Not verified: the exact study-count thresholds for each padlock level; secondary summaries seen for this entry gave slightly different numbers, so use the EEF's own toolkit guide.

**Cost.** Pound signs estimate the *additional* cost of delivering the approach for a class of pupils over a year (training, materials, staffing), in bands from very low to very high. Staff time already paid for is mostly not counted, which makes approaches that consume teacher time look cheaper than they feel.

**Illustrative headline figures** (seen in EEF pages and summaries during research for this entry; confirm on the live site):

| Strand | Impact | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Metacognition and self-regulation | +8 months (raised from +7 in the 2025 update) | Very low/low cost |
| Feedback | +6 months | Wide variation, including negative effects |
| Homework | +5 months | Stronger in secondary than primary |
| Collaborative learning approaches | +5 months | +6 in secondary |
| Mastery learning | +5 months | Higher in maths and science than reading |

## In the classroom

The Toolkit is not a teaching manual, but teachers and heads of department can use it well:

- **Start with a problem, not a strand.** 'Year 9 cannot plan extended writing' leads you to metacognition and to the linked guidance report; browsing for the biggest number does not.
- **Read the 'How could you implement in your setting?' and 'Behind the average' sections.** For homework, for example, the detail (linked to classwork, feedback given, not excessive) matters more than the +5.
- **Move from strand to guidance report.** The EEF's guidance reports (metacognition, feedback, literacy, maths, science) turn the broad strand into specific recommendations. See the entries on metacognition and self-regulation and on the EEF teacher feedback guidance.
- **Treat labels with suspicion.** 'Feedback' in the research included computer-generated hints and laboratory tasks; it is not evidence for any particular marking policy.

## For leaders

- Use the Toolkit to **rule out weak bets and shortlist promising ones**, then look for a specific, well-evaluated programme or a well-specified practice. The DfE's pupil premium guidance expects spending to be informed by evidence and points schools to the EEF.
- Weigh **all three ratings**. A +4 with four padlocks at very low cost can be a better decision than a +6 with one padlock.
- Low or negative averages are informative too: repeating a year and setting or streaming show little or no average benefit, with costs for some groups.
- Budget for **implementation**: most disappointing results in EEF trials reflect poor take-up or fidelity rather than a bad idea. Pair the Toolkit with the EEF implementation guidance.
- **Evaluate locally.** An average from other schools does not show that the approach worked in yours.

## Cautions and critiques

- **Averages hide variation.** The EEF itself warns that each headline conceals a wide spread, and that the same approach can harm in some conditions.
- **Combining unlike studies.** Critics including Adrian Simpson (Durham) argue that effect sizes depend on the test used, the sample and the comparison group, so averaging and ranking them across approaches is not valid. Terry Wrigley made a related criticism reported in Tes, noting that summing the months across strands gives an implausible total; the EEF's reply is that the figures are not additive and were never meant to be.
- **Strands are broad categories**, not interventions. 'Digital technology' or 'collaborative learning' cover very different practices.
- **Much of the evidence is not from English secondary schools**, and a good deal is older or from short studies with researcher-made tests.
- **League-table reading.** Sorting by months and adopting the top three is exactly the misuse the EEF's own blog on Toolkit risks warns against.
- **Attainment only.** Wellbeing, attendance, behaviour and enjoyment are largely outside its scope.
